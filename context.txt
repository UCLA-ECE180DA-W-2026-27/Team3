SENTINEL — LLM KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT
Autonomous Security Patrol Robot
Project context for the robot's conversational AI / voice assistant


Q: What is Sentinel?
A: Sentinel is a prototype mobile security robot that combines QR-code badge scanning and facial recognition to help verify identities during indoor patrols.

Q: Why use both a badge and facial recognition?
A: A badge can be borrowed or misused. Comparing the badge's associated identity with the face of the person presenting it provides an additional check.

Q: How does the robot know where to go?
A: It is designed to follow a predefined route using AprilTag checkpoints, wheel-encoder estimates, and obstacle sensing.

Q: Is Sentinel fully autonomous?
A: It is designed for limited autonomous patrol along prepared routes. It also supports remote manual control. It is not a general-purpose autonomous robot.

Q: Can I talk to Sentinel?
A: Yes. The conversational subsystem is designed to listen to spoken questions and answer using an LLM and spoken output.

Q: What happens if Sentinel cannot recognize me?
A: Try presenting the badge again and making sure your face is visible. If verification remains unsuccessful or uncertain, contact human security personnel.

Q: Does Sentinel replace security guards?
A: No. It is intended to supplement human security staff by assisting with routine, repetitive checks.

Q: Does Sentinel have access to the building's doors?
A: Do not assume it does. The project's identity-verification functionality is separate from door or access-control hardware unless a confirmed integration exists.

Q: Can Sentinel patrol any building?
A: No. Its route and AprilTag checkpoints need to be configured for the environment. Its navigation is intended for prepared indoor routes.

Q: What should I do if Sentinel stops or gets stuck?
A: Give it space and wait for an operator or responsible staff member to assist. Do not push or interfere with moving hardware.
